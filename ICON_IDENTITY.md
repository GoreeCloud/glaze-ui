# Glaze UI Application and Service Icon Identity System

Status: **Glaze UI 1.5 Stable**. Glaze UI 1.4.0 remains the mandatory Stable production target until 1.5 is promoted.

## Governing principle

The central identity rule is **shared DNA without shared identity**. Glaze UI supplies common ecosystem grammar while each product retains a distinct, memorable identity.

## Identity hierarchy

Every first-party product identity is evaluated across four layers:

**Ecosystem → Family → Product → State**

Ecosystem establishes Glaze UI coherence. Family establishes restrained relationship to related products. Product carries the unique identity users recognize. State remains temporary and must not replace or recolor away the stable product identity.

## Identity DNA and Identity Lock

Every major application and service requires an Identity DNA profile. The profile may record primary symbol, silhouette, geometric motif, directional structure, characteristic negative space, material personality, identity color family, secondary accent, depth behavior, and motion personality.

Identity DNA complements the existing Identity Lock. DNA describes the broader personality and family relationship; the Identity Lock records the features that must survive adaptive presentation and redesign.

## Ecosystem and family rules

The complete GoreeCloud ecosystem is one visual family, but product families may establish restrained secondary relationships. Family resemblance may come from material behavior, compositional rhythm, secondary geometry, lighting, depth, or motion personality. Family membership must never be implemented as the same symbol with different colors.

The Candidate defines these first-party families as governed categories: communication, productivity, media, cloud-and-data, network, security, system-utility, AI-and-intelligence, browser-and-discovery, and persistence-and-resilience.

Family assignments are review metadata rather than permission to flatten cross-family products into one visual stereotype. Products that participate in more than one conceptual domain still require one primary identity with any secondary relationship documented explicitly.

## Product color ownership

Each major product owns an identity color family rather than a single literal color. Identity color is subordinate to recognition by shape. Products that commonly appear together should not rely on nearly identical color families unless their silhouettes remain unmistakably distinct in monochrome.

Protected semantic colors remain outside product ownership. Product branding must never appropriate or redefine success, information, warning, danger, privacy, security, protected, restricted, online, offline, syncing, unavailable, or other system state semantics.

## Shape and negative-space ownership

Distinctive arrangements of geometry and negative space may become registered identity traits. Common primitives such as circles, pages, shields, clouds, and locks remain reusable, but neighboring products must not casually duplicate a product's distinctive silhouette, cutout, gap relationship, or characteristic arrangement when doing so would create confusion.

## Material, depth, and motion personality

Products may express different personalities using only Glaze UI-defined material, depth, and motion grammar. Security identities may use denser treatment, media may use richer luminosity, cloud products may use layered translucency, utilities may use quieter solids, and AI products may use controlled luminous depth. These are tendencies, not hard-coded visual stereotypes.

Motion personality is allowed only where motion communicates contextually meaningful state. Reduced-motion behavior remains mandatory.

## Service derivation

Associated services derive relationship from an application's Identity DNA without copying the application icon. A service may inherit one geometric motif, color relationship, or symbolic fragment while using the simpler service construction hierarchy.

Parent and child services may share a structural motif while child services add capability-specific geometry. Background services prioritize capability recognition over branding. Low-level infrastructure components use still simpler symbolic representation while retaining Glaze UI geometry, stroke, semantic color, and accessibility rules.

Temporary service states remain separate overlays or adjacent treatments. Running, stopped, degraded, updating, syncing, offline, warning, and failed states must not mutate the underlying service identity.

## Third-party and legacy identities

Third-party identities remain third-party identities. Glaze UI may normalize presentation through framing, padding, masks, environmental response, badges, selection, contrast, or accessibility treatment, but it must not impose GoreeCloud family motifs or falsely rebrand the asset.

Legacy icons may be presented inside an adaptive normalization surface when necessary. Preservation of recognizable identity is preferred over aggressive restyling.

## Identity review

Every major first-party identity is reviewed against the ecosystem, its primary family, neighboring products, associated services, monochrome presentation, optical-size reduction, and expected state overlays.

The review vocabulary includes recognition, distinctiveness, family relationship, Glaze coherence, scalability, accessibility, and longevity. These dimensions support consistent human review and must not be reduced to a single automated score that overrides design judgment.

## Collision detection

Identity collision detection is a review aid, not an autonomous rejection authority. Tooling may flag similarities in silhouette, dominant geometry, negative space, color distribution, or composition. Human review determines whether the similarity creates real user confusion.

## Ecosystem Wall and family preview

Family previews and an Ecosystem Wall may be produced as review artifacts using ordinary design tools, generated contact sheets, repository previews, or other suitable methods. They should contain major application and service identities at multiple optical sizes and expose repetitive geometry, color clustering, visual-weight imbalance, weak identities, and accidental hierarchy.

Glaze UI does not require or plan a dedicated Icon Studio application for this review. Automated collision analysis and Ecosystem Wall generation remain **Planned** only where their separate implementation would materially improve review quality; human review remains authoritative.

## New product creation and redesign

New product identity work begins with product meaning, differentiation, family relationship, reserved concepts, and a proposed long-term Identity Lock before rendering exploration begins. Concepts should be reduced to one dominant visual idea, with secondary motifs added only when they materially improve recognition.

Recognition precedes rendering polish. Beauty should emerge from structure, geometry, proportion, negative space, material relationships, light, and restrained color rather than decorative complexity.

Existing identities should not be replaced merely because visual trends change. If the Identity Lock remains effective, evolve materials, lighting, geometry refinement, or adaptive presentation first. Major identity replacement is reserved for confusing, inaccessible, excessively generic, technically limiting, or no-longer-representative identities. When replacement is necessary, preserve at least one strong recognizable relationship where practical.

## Application-to-service relationship

Application icons communicate destination and product identity. Service icons communicate capability and infrastructure. Shared DNA communicates ownership; different construction communicates role. Administrative surfaces must preserve this hierarchy so users can distinguish what they can launch from what operates beneath the system.

## Final identity questions

A successful Glaze UI identity system should allow an icon to answer four questions quickly:

1. What is this?
2. What family does it belong to?
3. What state is it in?
4. Does it belong to Glaze UI?

The system succeeds when those answers emerge from layered identity rather than repeated corporate logos or color-only differentiation.

## Candidate promotion boundary

This identity system is Candidate work. Source validation proves contract consistency, not rendered identity quality. Stable promotion requires representative ecosystem review, family review, monochrome and optical-size validation, accessibility review, state-overlay review, collision assessment, continuity assessment for redesigned identities, and confirmation that Planned review automation is not represented as implemented.
